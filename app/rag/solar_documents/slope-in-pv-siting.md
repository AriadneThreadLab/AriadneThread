# Why slope matters for photovoltaic siting

Terrain slope is a siting constraint and a construction-cost driver. This note
explains why methodologies treat slope as important. It does not publish a
numeric cut-off and must not be used as a replacement for SolarDSS slope
layers.

## Construction and earthworks

Steep ground increases earthworks, access difficulty, and foundation
complexity for ground-mounted arrays. Tracking systems and fixed-tilt tables
need relatively even grades so row spacing and drainage remain workable.
Installers also consider erosion risk and the stability of access tracks.
These are qualitative reasons that appear in siting guidance. The actual slope
value at a candidate is a geospatial measurement performed by the assessment
engine.

## Resource and shading geometry

Slope changes the local incidence geometry and can increase mutual shading
between rows when the grade faces away from the preferred orientation. Hill
shadows and ridgelines belong to terrain analysis, not to this narrative.
Method notes explain that slope is screened because it affects constructability
and array layout. They do not compute a slope angle for a named site.

## How to answer “why is slope important?”

Explain the construction, drainage, and shading rationale, then state that
whether a particular site fails a slope screen is a deterministic engine
result. Never invent a slope in degrees or a percentage grade from this
document. Cite this note as knowledge evidence and keep SolarDSS slope
indicators in the analytical evidence partition.
